PDF statement reconciliation worksheet

To reconcile a month from a PDF bank statement, check the statement against its own printed balances first, then match it to your ledger and list what hasn't cleared. This worksheet does both checks in one CSV: the first catches a bad extraction, the second ties the bank's closing balance to your book balance to the cent.

What are the two checks?

Check 1, the statement against itself. Printed opening balance + deposits − withdrawals must equal the printed closing balance. You do this on the lines you typed or converted out of the PDF, before anything goes into the books. If it's off, a line is missing, doubled or has the wrong sign, and no amount of matching later will fix it.

Check 2, the bank against the books. Printed closing balance + deposits in transit − outstanding checks must equal the book balance for that account on the statement date. This is the classic bank reconciliation, and it only means something once check 1 passes.

How do you use the worksheet?

  1. Copy the four printed numbers off the statement: opening balance, closing balance, and (if printed) total deposits and total withdrawals.
  2. Add up the lines you extracted and fill in step 1. The difference has to be 0.00. If it isn't, find the line before moving on.
  3. List everything in your ledger for that account that isn't on the statement yet: checks written but not cleared, deposits made on the last day.
  4. Fill in step 2 with your ledger balance on the statement date. The difference has to be 0.00.

FAQ

What if the statement doesn't tie to its own balances?

Stop and fix the extraction before you touch the books. A difference usually means one line was dropped, read twice, or read with the wrong sign, and the size of the difference points at it: a flipped sign leaves twice the amount, a missing line leaves exactly that amount.

What is an outstanding item?

Anything recorded in your books that the bank hasn't processed by the statement date: a check that hasn't cleared, or a deposit made on the last day that posts next month. They explain the gap between the bank's closing balance and your book balance.

Can I use this worksheet for a credit card statement?

Yes. Treat purchases as withdrawals and payments as deposits. The printed new balance plays the part of the closing balance, and the printed previous balance is the opening balance.
