README

Salon and spa bookkeeping kit

Bookkeeping for a salon or spa keeps card tips as money owed to staff, books booth rent as income, separates retail product sales from services, holds gift cards as a liability until they are used, and grosses each card deposit back up before processor fees. This kit has the accounts, a checklist and a worked month that ties.

Files

File What it is
chart-of-accounts.csv A starting chart of accounts for a salon or spa: service, retail and booth rent income, tips payable, gift cards outstanding, backbar supplies apart from retail cost. Columns: Number, Name, Type, Detail, Notes.
month-end-checklist.md Ten steps for closing a salon's month, from pulling card deposits apart to locking the period.
card-deposits.csv A fictional month of card deposits, one row per week, each grossed up into services, retail and tips with the processor fee on its own column.
october-2026-month.csv The same fictional salon's October 2026 on cash basis: every line, the account it goes to, and whether it counts in profit.
check_salon_month.py Checks that every deposit reads across, that tips payable nets to zero, that deposits agree with the month, and prints revenue, expenses and profit. Python 3, standard library only.

How to use it

  1. Import chart-of-accounts.csv into your accounting software, or use it as a list to compare with the accounts you already have. Delete booth rent if you have no renters and the gift card lines if you don't sell them.

  2. Each month, export the booking system's sales and tips summary and fill in a copy of card-deposits.csv from your processor statement.

  3. Run the check:

    python3 check_salon_month.py card-deposits.csv october-2026-month.csv

  4. Work through month-end-checklist.md before you lock the month.

The worked month

Juniper Hair Studio is a fictional salon with commission stylists and three booth renters. October 2026, cash basis:

Amount
Card services 28,400.00
Card retail 3,600.00
Cash services 1,850.00
Booth rent, 3 renters at 900.00 2,700.00
Revenue 36,550.00
Processor fees, 2.8% of 36,200.00 card volume 1,013.60
Retail restock 1,450.00
Backbar color and supplies 960.00
Stylist wages and commissions, gross 14,300.00
Employer payroll taxes 1,093.95
Rent 4,500.00
Expenses 23,317.55
Profit 13,232.45

Card tips of 4,200.00 came in with the card sales and went out through payroll, so they sit in tips payable and never touch profit. The card volume the processor charges on (36,200.00) includes those tips, which is why the fee is worked out on more than the card sales.

All figures are fictional.

FAQ

Are card tips salon revenue?

No. Tips added on a card belong to the staff member. They land in your account with the sale, sit in tips payable, and go out to staff through payroll. Booked as revenue, the month looks better than it was and payroll looks worse.

Is booth rent income for the salon?

Yes. Renters pay you to use a chair or room, and that rent is salon income, recorded apart from service sales. Whether a stylist is a renter or an employee depends on how the arrangement actually works; that is a question for your accountant.

When does a gift card become revenue?

When it is redeemed. A gift card sold in December is a service you owe later, so it sits in gift cards outstanding until the client uses it. Prepaid packages and memberships work the same way.
