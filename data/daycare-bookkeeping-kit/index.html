README

Daycare bookkeeping kit

Bookkeeping for a daycare records parent tuition, state subsidy payments and CACFP food reimbursements as separate income, matches each subsidy deposit to the children and weeks it covers, books payroll from the payroll report, and keeps what each family paid so year-end statements are right. This kit has the accounts, a checklist and two small scripts.

Files

File What it is
chart-of-accounts.csv A starting chart of accounts for a licensed center: tuition, subsidy, food program and fees as separate income, prepaid tuition as a liability. Columns: Number, Name, Type, Detail, Notes.
month-end-checklist.md Eight monthly steps plus the January job of parent year-end statements.
subsidy-remittance.csv A fictional subsidy remittance: 14 children, four weeks each, weekly rate and amount per child.
september-2026-month.csv A fictional center's September 2026 on cash basis: every line, its account, and whether it counts in profit.
check_subsidy_month.py Checks each child's line on the remittance, ties the remittance to the subsidy deposit, and prints revenue by source and profit.
family-payments.csv A fictional year of payments for three families, one row per payment.
family_year_end.py Totals each family's payments for the year, by type, for parent year-end statements.

Both scripts are Python 3, standard library only.

How to use it

  1. Import chart-of-accounts.csv into your accounting software, or compare it with the accounts you have. Home daycares use the same income lines and add home expense accounts.

  2. Each month, fill in a copy of subsidy-remittance.csv from the remittance notice and run:

    python3 check_subsidy_month.py subsidy-remittance.csv september-2026-month.csv

  3. Keep every parent payment in a file shaped like family-payments.csv, or export one from your child care software. In January run:

    python3 family_year_end.py family-payments.csv 2026

    Fill in your center's name, address and taxpayer ID at the top of the script first.

  4. Work through month-end-checklist.md before you lock each month.

The worked month

Little Acorn Learning Center is a fictional licensed center with 48 children enrolled. September 2026, cash basis:

Amount
Parent tuition, card and ACH 38,700.00
State subsidy deposit, 14 children 11,240.00
CACFP reimbursement for August meals 2,165.00
Registration fees, 3 new families 450.00
Revenue 52,555.00
Payroll, gross wages 29,800.00
Employer payroll taxes 2,279.70
Groceries 3,980.00
Classroom supplies 1,120.00
Rent 7,500.00
Liability insurance 890.00
Card and ACH fees 420.00
Expenses 45,989.70
Profit 6,565.30

The 2,165.00 food deposit pays for August's meals, while September's 3,980.00 of groceries will be reimbursed in October. That is why one month's food line never looks right on its own. Compare food cost with food income over a quarter instead.

The three families in family-payments.csv are a separate small sample, not Little Acorn's 48 children.

All figures are fictional.

Home daycares

A home daycare uses part of the house for the business. The IRS lets daycare providers work out a time-space percentage: the share of the home used for child care times the share of hours it is used. Rent or mortgage interest, utilities and insurance are then deducted at that percentage (IRS Publication 587). The books keep the household bills and a log of hours; your accountant does the calculation.

FAQ

Why does the subsidy deposit need to be matched to children?

It arrives as one deposit for many children, usually after attendance is submitted. Without the split by child and week, you can't tell which families still owe their copay and which weeks are still unpaid.

What do parents need at year end?

Families claiming a dependent care FSA or the child care credit (Form 2441) need what they paid and the provider's name, address and taxpayer ID. That only works if tuition was recorded by family all year.

Is a nonprofit child care center different?

Yes. A nonprofit center also tracks grants and restricted funds and files a Form 990.
