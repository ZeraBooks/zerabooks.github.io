README

Therapist bookkeeping kit

Bookkeeping for a therapy practice records insurance payments and self-pay sessions separately, grosses card payments back up before processor fees, books late-cancellation fees as revenue, pays contract clinicians their agreed share of what they collect, and keeps owner pay out of expenses. This kit has the chart of accounts, a checklist and a worked month.

Files

File What it is
chart-of-accounts.csv A starting chart of accounts for a solo or group practice: insurance, self-pay and cancellation fees as separate income, contract clinician payments, supervision and practice costs. Columns: Number, Name, Type, Detail, Notes.
month-end-checklist.md Eight steps for closing a practice's month. No clinical records needed.
insurance-remittances.csv A fictional month of insurance EFTs, each matched to its remittance total.
clinician-collections.csv The same month's collections by clinician, split into insurance, self-pay sessions and late-cancel fees, with each contract clinician's split rate.
october-2026-month.csv The fictional practice's October 2026 on cash basis: every line, its account, and whether it counts in profit.
check_practice_month.py Ties EFTs to remittances and to collections, works out each contract clinician's pay, and prints profit before owner pay. Python 3, standard library only.

How to use it

  1. Import chart-of-accounts.csv into your accounting software, or compare it with the accounts you have. A solo practice can delete the clinician lines.

  2. Each month, fill in copies of insurance-remittances.csv from your remittance advice and clinician-collections.csv from the practice software's payments report.

  3. Run the check:

    python3 check_practice_month.py insurance-remittances.csv clinician-collections.csv october-2026-month.csv

  4. Work through month-end-checklist.md before you lock the month.

The worked month

Harborlight Counseling is a fictional practice: the owner and two contract clinicians paid 60% of what they collect. October 2026, cash basis:

Amount
Insurance EFTs, 4 payers 9,860.00
Self-pay card payments for sessions 5,940.00
Late-cancellation fees 300.00
Revenue 16,100.00
Card processor fees, 2.9% of 6,240.00 180.96
Contract clinicians, 60% of the 7,400.00 they collected 4,440.00
Practice management and telehealth software 149.00
Office rent 1,600.00
Liability insurance 62.00
Continuing education course 295.00
Expenses 6,726.96
Profit before owner pay 9,373.04

The clinicians' 4,440.00 is worked out from their collections that month, which is why the books need payments by clinician, not just a total. What the owner takes home comes out of the 9,373.04 as a draw or salary, depending on how the practice is set up.

All figures are fictional.

FAQ

Is a superbill a receivable for the practice?

No. An out-of-network client pays you in full and sends the superbill to their insurer. Any reimbursement goes to the client, so nothing more is owed to the practice.

Are contract clinicians paid on sessions or collections?

In a split arrangement, usually on what they collected. A session that was billed but not paid yet isn't in the split until the money arrives. Whether a clinician is a contractor or an employee depends on how the work is actually arranged, and your accountant should decide it.

Can a solo therapist keep the books in a spreadsheet?

It works while the practice is small and you reconcile to the bank every month. A second payer panel or a clinician on a split is usually when it gets hard to keep straight.
