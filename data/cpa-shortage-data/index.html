CPA shortage data

U.S. schools awarded 55,152 accounting bachelor's and master's degrees in 2023-24, down 6.6%, while BLS projects about 115,300 accountant and auditor openings a year through 2035. NASBA counted 650,667 actively licensed CPAs in August 2026. This repository has those figures and the rest of the pipeline data in one CSV, each linked to its AICPA, NASBA or BLS source.

How to use it

  1. Open data/statistics.csv in Excel or Google Sheets. Filter on the id prefix for one group: pipeline, exam, workforce, firms or licensure.
  2. Check unit and period before quoting. Enrollment is counted by spring term; degrees by academic year.
  3. Quote the original source next to each number, then cite this collection.

Openings against graduates

One rough way to see the gap. This comparison is ours, not a published ratio.

Measure Figure Source

Openings include replacing people who retire or change jobs, and not every opening is filled by an accounting graduate, so the gap is not a precise shortage count.

Notes on the data

  • Every figure was checked against its primary source on November 4, 2026.
  • A licensed CPA and an employed accountant are different counts. NASBA counts licenses; BLS counts people working in the occupation. There is no official count of CPAs by age.
  • The BLS CPS 2025 annual averages leave out October.
  • The 55 and over shares are our math from the BLS table and say so in the notes column. None of these are ZeraBooks figures.
