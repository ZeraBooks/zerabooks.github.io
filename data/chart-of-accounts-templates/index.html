README

Chart of accounts templates

A small business chart of accounts is the numbered list of every account the books post to, grouped by type: assets, liabilities, equity, income, cost of sales and operating expenses. Each industry then adds a few lines of its own, such as a trust account and the matching client liability for a law firm. The CSVs here give that layout for seven industries.

Each file uses the same five columns (Number, Name, Type, Detail, Notes) and the same number ranges.

Range Type
1000s Assets
2000s Liabilities
3000s Equity or net assets
4000s Income
5000s Cost of sales
6000s Operating expenses
Folder Built around Guide
nonprofits/ Gifts split by donor restriction, expenses split by function for Form 990 Part IX Bookkeeping for nonprofits
restaurants/ Food and beverage sales and cost, delivery apps, tips and sales tax held for others Bookkeeping for restaurants
law-firms/ The trust account and the client liability it must equal, legal fees by type Bookkeeping for law firms and trust accounts
contractors/ Job costs on the lines you bid, retainage, over and under billings Bookkeeping for contractors
startups/ SAFEs kept out of revenue, deferred revenue, payroll split by team Bookkeeping for startups
property-management/ Trust and operating accounts, owner funds and tenant deposits held Bookkeeping for property management
trucking/ Linehaul, fuel surcharge and accessorial income, per-mile costs, fuel tax payable, optional factoring lines Bookkeeping for trucking

Each folder's README explains the lines that are specific to that industry. The account names are generic and contain no real company data.

How to use these files

  1. Open the industry's chart-of-accounts.csv in a spreadsheet.
  2. Delete the lines you don't need and rename the placeholders the folder README points out.
  3. Import the rest into your accounting software. If it uses five-digit numbers, add a zero to each.
