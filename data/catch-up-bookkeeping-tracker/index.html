README

Catch-up bookkeeping tracker

Catch-up bookkeeping fills in months that were never recorded. You start from the last month whose closing balance you trust, gather every bank and card statement since then, and work the months oldest first, reconciling each one before the next. This repo has a month-by-month tracker for every account, a script that lists missing statement months and months that don't tie, and a catch-up checklist, with a fictional business nine months behind.

Files

File What it is
catch-up-tracker-template.csv A blank tracker. One row per account per month: statement received, printed opening and closing, imported, reconciled, books closing, notes.
catch-up-tracker.csv The fictional example below, two days into its catch-up.
catch_up_status.py Reads the tracker and lists missing statements, breaks in the balance chain, months that don't tie, and the next month to work on for each account.
catch-up-checklist.md The order of work, what to gather, and when the catch-up is done.

The script is Python 3, standard library only.

How to use it

  1. For each account, put the last month you trust as its first row, with its closing balance.

  2. Add a row for every month since then as statements come in. Copy the opening and closing balances printed on the statement.

  3. As you record and reconcile each month, mark Imported and Reconciled and fill in the closing balance your books show.

  4. Run:

    python3 catch_up_status.py catch-up-tracker.csv

    Add a month (for example 2026-09) to say how far the catch-up should go. Without it, the script uses the latest month in the file.

  5. Work the "Next" line for each account, then run it again.

The worked example

Halden Bike Repair is a fictional shop with a checking account, a savings account and a Visa card. Its books were last right at December 31, 2025. In October 2026 the owner sent nine months of statements, January to September. Two days in, the tracker shows this for checking:

Month Printed opening Printed closing Status
December 2025 (last trusted) 22,418.60 Starting point
January 22,418.60 19,902.15 Reconciled
February 19,902.15 21,355.40 Reconciled
March 21,355.40 18,770.95 Books show 18,686.95, 84.00 short
April not sent not sent Missing
May 20,114.30 23,006.80 Not started
June 23,006.80 21,447.12 Not started
July 21,447.12 24,980.55 Not started
August 24,980.55 22,615.09 Not started
September 22,615.09 25,302.44 Not started

What the script reports:

  • Checking: April is missing. May opens at 20,114.30, which is 1,343.35 above where March closed, so that is April's net change. March doesn't tie: the books are 84.00 below the statement, the size of one card fee entered twice. Fix March before anything else.
  • Savings: every statement is in and January to March are reconciled. Next is April.
  • Visa: July isn't in the file at all. August opens at 2,904.66 against June's 3,377.51, so July's net change is -472.85. January and February are reconciled; next is March.

In all, 27 account-months need to be caught up, 7 are reconciled and 20 are to go. Both missing statements were requested from the bank the same day.

The business and figures are fictional.

FAQ

Where do you start when you are months behind?

At the most recent month whose closing balance in the books matches the bank. If no month ties, the job is a cleanup, not a catch-up, because months that were done wrong have to be fixed before new ones are added on top.

Do I need receipts to catch up?

Not to start. Bank and card statements carry every transaction and the balances that prove each month. Receipts and invoices answer category questions, and the questions list tells you which ones matter.

What deadlines does being behind put at risk?

For 2026, individual estimated tax is due April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027. Form 1099-NEC is due to contractors and the IRS by January 31. Both need current books, and so does any lender or landlord asking for a recent profit and loss.
