README

Real estate agent bookkeeping kit

A real estate agent should run a separate business bank account, record each commission at its gross amount with the brokerage split and fees as expenses, log every business mile, and set money aside for quarterly estimated tax. This kit has a chart of accounts, an expense category list, a commission split check that ties each closing to the brokerage's deposit, a mileage log, and a fictional agent's month.

Files

File What it is
chart-of-accounts.csv A starting chart for an agent or small team: checking, tax savings, card, owner draws, gross commission, split and fees, operating expenses. Columns: Number, Name, Type, Detail, Notes.
expense-categories.csv Common agent costs and the account each one goes to, with notes on gifts, referral fees and personal spending.
closings-october-2026.csv Two fictional closings: sale price, commission rate, your side, split rate, fees and the deposit received.
check_closings.py Works out your side, the split and the expected deposit for each closing, ties it to the deposit, and prints the gross entry.
october-2026-month.csv A fictional agent's October 2026: every line, its account, and whether it counts in profit.
mileage-log-october-2026.csv The same month's trips: date, from, to, purpose, miles, and whether it is business or commuting.
check_agent_month.py Prints the month's profit by section, lists draws and tax savings apart, and totals business miles.

Both scripts are Python 3, standard library only.

How to use it

  1. Import chart-of-accounts.csv into your accounting software, or compare it with the accounts you have. Use expense-categories.csv when you are not sure where a cost goes.

  2. For each closing, copy the figures from the commission statement or disbursement sheet into a file shaped like closings-october-2026.csv and run:

    python3 check_closings.py closings-october-2026.csv

    If the deposit doesn't tie, the commission statement has a deduction you haven't recorded yet.

  3. Keep the mileage log as you drive, not in April. At month end run:

    python3 check_agent_month.py october-2026-month.csv mileage-log-october-2026.csv

Dana also moved 6,000.00 to tax savings and took a 5,000.00 owner draw. Neither is an expense, so neither is in profit.

The three closing gifts are booked at their full 360.00 with each buyer's name in the memo. The IRS limits the deduction to $25 per person per year (IRS Publication 463), so 75.00 is deductible. Your tax preparer applies the limit.

The mileage log shows 183 business miles: showings, an open house, a listing appointment, the inspection and appraisal, the two closings and placing a listing sign. The 18 miles from home to the brokerage office on regular office days are commuting and are left out.

The agent, properties and figures are fictional.

FAQ

Is a real estate agent self-employed for taxes?

Almost always. The IRS treats licensed real estate agents as self-employed for federal tax purposes when nearly all their pay comes from sales and a written contract says they aren't employees. No tax is withheld from commissions, and the brokerage reports what it paid on Form 1099-NEC.

Should I record my commission gross or net?

Gross. Record your side of the commission as income and the brokerage split and fees as expenses. Profit comes out the same either way, but gross books show what the brokerage costs you and match the 1099-NEC whichever way it is reported.

When is estimated tax due?

For 2026 income: April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. On top of income tax, self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security, 2.9% Medicare) once net earnings reach $400. Your tax preparer sets the amounts; current books tell them the profit so far.
