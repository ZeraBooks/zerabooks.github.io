README

Owner-operator bookkeeping kit

An owner-operator should keep books every week: one business bank account, every settlement booked at its gross amount with each deduction as its own expense, fuel receipts kept for IFTA, and the bank account reconciled to its statement each month. From those books you can work out cost per mile, the number that tells you which loads to take.

This kit is the small set of files a one-truck owner-operator needs to do that from the cab.

Files

File What it is
chart-of-accounts.csv Ten starter accounts, plus five to add when you need them (truck loan, owner draws, business card, truck asset, 2290 savings). Start with ten, not sixty.
weekly-routine.md The five weekly steps and the monthly ones, as tick boxes.
weekly-log.csv One row per week to mark each step done and note gross, deductions and net. The first row is a filled example.
cost-per-mile.csv One month of miles, revenue and costs for a fictional one-truck owner-operator, Ridgeway Transport.
cost_per_mile.py Reads that file and prints revenue, cost and what is left per mile. Python 3, standard library only.
cost-per-mile-sheet.csv The same month with spreadsheet formulas, for people who would rather open it in Excel or Google Sheets.
records-to-keep.md Which records to keep, how often and why, with a folder layout.

How to use it

  1. Open a business checking account and use one card for the truck. Import chart-of-accounts.csv into your bookkeeping software or a spreadsheet.

  2. Every week, work through weekly-routine.md and mark the row in weekly-log.csv.

  3. At month end, put your ELD miles and the month's totals into cost-per-mile.csv and run:

    python3 cost_per_mile.py cost-per-mile.csv

    Or open cost-per-mile-sheet.csv and type over column B.

Worked example: cost per mile

Ridgeway Transport is a fictional one-truck owner-operator. In September 2026 its ELD showed 9,800 miles and its settlements paid 22,050.00 gross, which is 2.25 a mile.

Cost from the books September Per mile
Fuel 6,370.00 0.650
Insurance 1,480.00 0.151
Maintenance and tires 1,150.00 0.117
Truck loan interest 610.00 0.062
Permits, plates and Form 2290 set-aside 420.00 0.043
ELD and phone 95.00 0.010
Total cost 10,125.00 1.033

At 2.25 a mile coming in and 1.03 going out, Ridgeway keeps about 1.22 a mile, or 11,925.00 for the month, before depreciation, owner pay and income tax. Only the interest part of the truck payment is a cost; the principal pays down the loan. A rate below your cost per mile loses money however many miles you run.

All figures are fictional.

FAQ

Can an owner-operator keep books in a spreadsheet?

Yes, if you book settlements gross and reconcile to the bank each month. Most spreadsheets fail on the second part: nothing checks that the rows add up to the statement balance.

Do I record per diem in my books?

No. Per diem is a tax calculation from your days away from home. The books record what you actually spent; your accountant applies the rate.

Is bookkeeping different if I run under my own authority?

Yes. Leased-on drivers get one settlement from the carrier with deductions taken out. With your own authority you invoice brokers or shippers, chase payments or factor invoices, and pay insurance and permits yourself, so you also track receivables and factoring fees. A fuller chart for that is in chart-of-accounts-templates/trucking.
