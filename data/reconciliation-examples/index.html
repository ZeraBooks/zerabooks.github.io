README

Reconciliation examples

A reconciliation proves the books against an outside record, usually a bank statement: the opening balance plus every transaction has to equal the printed closing balance, and any difference has to be explained by a specific item, such as a check not yet cashed. Each folder here works through one kind of reconciliation with a small fictional data file that adds up exactly.

Folder What it shows Guide
proof-of-cash/ A four-column proof of cash template and a script that checks it Proof of cash, the four-column bank reconciliation
scanned-statement-misreads/ How one misread amount on a scanned statement shows up as a gap against the printed closing balance Converting a scanned bank statement to Excel
divorce-disclosure-timeline/ One month of statements laid out for a financial disclosure, tied to opening and closing balances Organizing bank statements for a divorce
cleanup-statement-grid/ A grid of accounts by month showing which statements are in, tied or off, worked oldest month first How a bookkeeping cleanup works
provider-switch-tie-out/ Old books' balances at a cutover date tied to the new opening balances, with one uncleared check explained Moving your books after Bench
bookkeeping-workload-estimate/ Monthly bookkeeping hours estimated from statement line counts plus fixed jobs How much a bookkeeper costs
combined-statements-continuity/ A year of statements combined into one file, with a missing month and an overlapping export found from the balances and dates Combining bank statements into one Excel file
bank-reconciliation-steps/ One month with a deposit in transit, two outstanding checks, a fee, interest and a transposed amount, both sides tying to 18,396.75 How to do a bank reconciliation
general-ledger-month/ Six journal entries turned into a trial balance that balances at 19,020.00 What is a general ledger?
sales-tax-tie-out/ Three months of sales tax payable rolled forward to the cent, with sales times rate catching a 4.50 shortfall in June Month-end close checklist

All figures are fictional.

How to use these files

  • Open the CSV in a folder next to its README; the README says which figure to replace and what each row proves.
  • Keep the layout and swap in your own amounts. The checks in each README work the same on real data.
  • To check a proof of cash in this layout, run python3 proof-of-cash/check_proof.py proof-of-cash/proof-of-cash-template.csv (Python 3, standard library only).
  • To check a bank reconciliation, run python3 bank-reconciliation-steps/check_reconciliation.py bank-reconciliation-steps/bank-reconciliation.csv.
  • To build a trial balance from journal entries, run python3 general-ledger-month/check_trial_balance.py general-ledger-month/journal.csv.
  • To check combined statements for a missing month or an overlap, run python3 combined-statements-continuity/check_continuity.py combined-statements-continuity/statements.csv combined-statements-continuity/overlap-lines.csv.
